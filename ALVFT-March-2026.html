The Alabama State Department of Education (SDE) invites you to participate in our popular monthly Virtual Field Trips. Alabama has been a trailblazer using technology for the purpose of advancing education. Alabama Connecting Classrooms, Educators, and Students Statewide (ACCESS), Alabama Learning Exchange (ALEX), Alabama Technology in Motion (ATiM), and e-Learning for Educators are all strong tools used for the advancement of digital learning, in addition to the expansive ways technology is used in the classroom.

Joining via Zoom: Create a free account and download the Zoom app, or go to zoom.us and click “Join a Meeting.” Check with your technology coordinator about displaying on a TV or large screen. A stand-alone mic may be needed if your computer does not have one. For testing or questions, contact the Alabama Supercomputer Help Desk: 1-800-338-8320.

Session Descriptions

Colors of the Sea: Thanks to the Greenies!
Kyle Halstead, Discovery Hall Programs Educator — Dauphin Island Sea Lab
All Grades
March 5 — 9:00–10:00 AM (K–5 focus) March 5 — 10:15–11:15 AM (Middle & High School focus)

Why is the ocean blue? When is it not blue? If it’s brown, pinkish, whitish, or green, what does that tell us? The ocean is full of living things that can give it color. Join us to chat about ocean color and the processes and plankton that can give it color. You’ll discover why we owe our existence to some very small green things in the ocean and why the ocean is truly a pot of gold for humans!

The 9:00 AM session is geared toward younger students (K–5); the 10:15 AM session is more suitable for middle and high school students.

Movement and Motion
Jerry Csaki, Senior Director of Education and Leadership, Pro Football Hall of Fame
Grades 5–12
March 18 — 10:15–11:15 AM March 26 — 9:00–10:00 AM

This program examines the relationship of science and the game of professional football. Straight-line movement, zig-zags, circular motion, back & forth, pushing & pulling — all movements that influence the sport. This 50-minute, highly interactive program is supplemented by a lesson aligned with Common Core/National Standards of Education.

A Neighborhood Hero: Georgia Gilmore and the Civil Rights Movement
Jake Smith, Education Curator, Alabama Department of Archives & History
Grades 3–6
March 19 — 9:00–10:00 AM March 19 — 10:15–11:15 AM March 25 — 9:00–10:00 AM March 25 — 10:15–11:15 AM

You’ve heard of Rosa Parks and Martin Luther King Jr. — but this presentation covers one of the lesser-known names of the Montgomery Bus Boycott. Join us to hear about Georgia Gilmore: a mother, a chef, and an example of the stories under the surface of our shared history.

Students will learn about:

  • Georgia Gilmore’s early life
  • The importance of foodways and domestic work to social movements
  • The boycott as an expression of community
  • Gilmore’s continued community work
Alabama Standards
  • SS24.5.12a — Describe ways in which the Civil Rights Movement sought to create change.
  • SS24.5.12c — Describe the contributions of Alabama leaders and foot soldiers of the Civil Rights Movement as well as other Alabamian supporters who aided their efforts.
  • SS24.5.12e — Identify Alabamians who contributed to concurrent civil rights movements in the United States during the decades following World War II.
A Journey Into the Life of Helen Keller
Keller Johnson-Thompson, Vice President, Helen Keller Foundation for Research and Education
Grades K–5
March 24 — 9:00–10:00 AM

Keller Johnson-Thompson, great-grandniece of Helen Keller, takes students on a marvelous journey into the amazing life of Helen Keller — America’s First Lady of Courage. From her early days in Tuscumbia, Alabama to her travels around the world, Helen Keller not only lived a wonderful and full life despite being deaf and blind, but she also inspired others to make a positive difference in the world.

Through this journey, students will learn about Helen Keller’s life and, more importantly, come to understand the importance of good values and character — as Helen Keller herself demonstrated.

Let’s Explore Your Eyes and How They Work
Keller Johnson-Thompson, Vice President, Helen Keller Foundation for Research and Education
Grades 1–6
March 24 — 10:15–11:15 AM

Of all our senses, vision is perhaps the most important. This virtual field trip takes you into the world of vision as we explore how our eyes work, first aid for eye injuries, and vision problems that may occur. We will also explore the eyes of different animals — who has the best sight, who has the worst, and why.

Careers in the NFL
Jerry Csaki, Senior Director of Education and Leadership, Pro Football Hall of Fame
Grades 5–12
March 18 — 9:00–10:00 AM March 26 — 10:15–11:15 AM

This program examines the importance of education and the hundreds of careers available in and around the National Football League. A 10-minute career video examining a wide variety of careers with the San Francisco 49ers organization is shown during the session. The 50-minute, highly interactive program is supplemented by a lesson aligned with Common Core/National Standards of Education.
